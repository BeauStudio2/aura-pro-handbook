How to use the shared model

Aura Pro turns an objective and evidence about the world into possible courses of action. It helps people choose, coordinate the work, check what happened, and improve what happens next. The capabilities share one connected account of the undertaking, while preserving different views of what is known, planned, authorized, and still hypothetical.

AP maintains a connected picture of what exists, what is wanted, what work could achieve it, what that work depends on, and what the evidence supports. Changing one part lets AP identify which decisions and outcomes need another look.

Five views explain how the system fits together:

  • Mission Logic explains how people and AP decide which question or action comes next, including when to investigate, act, wait, stop, switch methods, or reframe.
  • The shared model keeps what AP knows, assumes, and connects available to that reasoning.
  • The capabilities observe, calculate, communicate, coordinate, and check the work.
  • Authority establishes who can turn a proposed response into a commitment or instruction.
  • Business management connects objectives, affected parties, costs, benefits, performance measures, and decisions across the portfolio.

The architecture makes Mission Logic executable through the shared model and cooperating capabilities. The shared rules keep evidence, forecasts, decisions, and authorized actions connected across the platform.

One system across scales

The common structure is an objective, the conditions relevant to it, possible methods, resources, decisions, actions, and evidence of the result. A sink repair may need one person, a few observations, and a functional check. A development can require thousands of linked packages, financial and physical models, and many people with different responsibilities.

Complexity follows the work. A person fixing a sink does not need to create a company, project hierarchy, budget, or approval chain. A worker on a large development may need only the current instruction, a way to report an exception, and the required check. The same person may set the objective, perform the work, and accept its result.

Construction is the first client application. Its equipment, production methods, contracts, and acceptance rules are domain knowledge attached to the common model. A car wash contributes different routines and equipment behavior. A restoration contributes different methods and owner preferences. The shared structure must accommodate those differences without making construction terminology compulsory.

Aura Pro supports designing solutions, planning from the current state to the intended outcome, coordinating execution, observing unanticipated conditions, learning with the team, and retaining knowledge through operation and later projects. Existing platforms remain usable while the client chooses which functions AP takes on directly.

One change across the system

Follow the procurement example. The original air handler is reported late. This is one event entering a shared model, not a separate copy of the problem for each capability.

  1. Receive and establish the finding. Telemetry receives the supplier update. Evidence checks the order, unit, revision, and meaning of the date. The supplier has changed a forecast; the equipment has not arrived.
  2. Trace the consequence. The project model connects the unit to installation, connections, testing, and occupancy. Inquiry identifies the decisions still open and the last useful time to preserve an alternative.
  3. Develop alternatives. Planning compares retaining the original, buying an assembled substitute, and using a sectional alternative. Logistics supplies actual offers and resource availability. Design and commercial questions become assigned inquiries.
  4. Acquire discriminating evidence. The preferred assembled substitute depends on access. Telemetry requests a survey; Evidence establishes that the proposed route fails. The corrected geometry changes the project model and the affected comparisons.
  5. Revise the whole strategy. Planning recalculates design work, procurement, cash timing, shared resources, testing, and occupancy. It provisionally prefers the sectional alternative. The interface shows the recommendation and what still needs confirmation.
  6. Commit and release separately. Authority identifies the people who can approve the purchase and funding. Logistics establishes the resulting commitments. Once the installation prerequisites are satisfied, the superintendent releases the current instruction through Execution. The foreman acknowledges it.
  7. Check the work and learn. Observations supply actual quantities, effort, waiting, and results. Verification records what passed. Learning compares those results with the saved predictions, while the project model and Planning update the remaining development forecast.

The loop can continue while independent work proceeds. A failed check can open correction work and a new inquiry at the same time. A late report can correct history without pretending the team knew it earlier. A new forecast can reach the scheduler immediately, while changing a crew instruction still requires the applicable decision and release.

The shared model

In the procurement example, the supplier update, the equipment it concerns, the installation method, and the occupancy requirement are connected. The planner can trace why a late unit matters; the foreman can see which measurement would change the choice. They are using different views of the same undertaking.

AP preserves those connections with stable identities, dated records, and explicit relationships. Each capability refers to the same work, asset, finding, or decision. A new interpretation retains the sources and earlier versions it depends on. The following terms distinguish the parts of that shared picture.

ConceptMeaning and responsibility
Objective and obligationThe desired outcome or condition, who owns it, success criteria, priorities, and constraints. A finite obligation can close; a standing obligation continues across recurring work.
Project and operationA project coordinates a finite larger outcome. An operation sustains a condition over time and creates recurring work or exceptions. A portfolio groups undertakings without duplicating their work.
Mission and taskA Mission is a bounded outcome with an accountable coordinator and completion criteria. A task is an executable activity within it. A Mission can stand alone. Each task has one owning Mission and an identified executor when assigned.
Work packageA planning and calculation boundary: a defined scope, required results, available methods, prerequisites, and resources. Packages can nest and coordinate work across Missions. A package coordinator owns cross-Mission coordination; the Mission coordinators retain their responsibilities.
Asset, place, and resourceWhat the work concerns and what it uses: a sink, floor, vehicle, system, person, crew, material, machine, space, or utility. Resource records carry capacity, condition, calendars, and the rules for use or consumption.
Source, observation, and claimA source preserves an original record or testimony. An observation records what was encountered or measured. A claim states what a source or analysis asserts, with its scope, time, uncertainty, and supporting evidence.
Finding and acceptanceA finding states what the evidence supports and leaves unresolved. Acceptance records who judged that a particular criterion was met, for which scope and revision. Neither implies unrelated work has passed.
Question and methodA question identifies an answer owed, its recipient, decision consequence, and useful deadline. A method specifies how to obtain the answer or perform the work. Methods can be compared, replaced, or composed.
Behavior model and scenarioA behavior model calculates how conditions and actions affect progress, resources, quality, or physical state. A scenario combines a dated starting state, proposed choices, external conditions, and model versions into a possible future.
Proposal and decisionA proposal presents a particular course and its implications. A decision records the authorized person's choice about that exact scope and revision, including conditions and rationale.
Grant, reservation, and commitmentA grant permits specified actions. A reservation allocates capacity or an allowance under stated rules. A commitment binds a party or resource. Budget, delegated authority, and available cash are distinct.
Interest, economic effect, and measureAn interest links a party to an objective or obligation. An economic effect identifies who pays, benefits, or carries exposure and when. A measure defines its calculation, scope, time basis, sources, and owner; targets, forecasts, and actual results remain distinct.
Instruction and execution eventAn instruction tells an authorized executor what to do. Issued, acknowledged, started, interrupted, performed, and accepted are separate events. Reported performance is not automatically accepted performance.

Accountability and planning are different views. A Mission names who owes an outcome; a work package defines how a scope is calculated and coordinated. A task has at most one primary leaf package for planning. Other floor, trade, system, and contract views reference that task instead of creating copies. A simple Mission can use tasks directly without exposing a package hierarchy.

One example of the different views: the Mission “make ventilation ready for testing” names the accountable coordinator and required outcome. Its “install the sectional air handler” work package organizes movement, assembly, connections, resources, and checks. “Join the modules” is a task within that work. A floor view and a mechanical-system view both reference that same task; neither creates another installation or another labor charge.

A parent package aggregates distinct child work and explicit parent-level work, such as an integrated system test. One result can satisfy several requirements, but its underlying cost or installed quantity is counted once. Any split allocation must reconcile to the source total. Physical location, organizational membership, and work hierarchy remain separate relationships.

Relationships carry meaning. “Contains this work” does not mean “must finish first.” AP distinguishes physical prerequisites, required acceptance, regulatory conditions, resource competition, contractual restrictions, and preferred sequences. Evidence supporting a claim is different from a causal relationship predicting an effect. Each consequential relationship carries its source or assumption and the scope in which it applies.

Project memory is a retrievable view of these same sources, events, questions, and decisions, not a separate account of what happened. Inquiry records preserve why a case was investigated and what its answer settled. Lesson reviews preserve the evidence, counterexamples, scope, reviewer, disposition, and rationale. Links connect each adopted lesson to the model or practice revisions it informed.

The engineering basis in the shared model

The engineering basis connects the required result to the physical configuration, interfaces, calculations, and evidence that support it. It explains why a solution can work, under which conditions, and what still needs establishing. It is part of the shared model used by the existing eleven capabilities, not a separate approval system or a twelfth capability.

AP traces a requirement through the assets and interfaces that satisfy it, the behavior model used to assess them, the installation and operating conditions, and the checks that establish the result. A reviewer’s acceptance records responsibility and scope; the underlying evidence preserves the technical reason. A person working alone can supply the same reasoning through a simple observation and functional check.

Connected recordWhat it contains and why it matters
Requirement and operating conditionsThe real need, measurable criterion, units, intended use, relevant loads or environments, and owner. Meeting an isolated component criterion may leave the parent system’s need unresolved.
Configuration and applicabilityWhich asset revisions, installed arrangement, temporary works, settings, location, and time interval a conclusion covers. The finished design and a construction-stage configuration can have different capabilities.
Physical and functional interfacesThe endpoints and compatible ranges for geometry, tolerances, loads, movement, power, flow, control signals, and access. An interface links both owners and relevant checks; it is more than a predecessor arrow between tasks.
Behavior and calculationThe governing relationship or specialist model, inputs and their evidence status, boundary conditions, assumptions, validity range, version, and reproducible outputs. Physical networks can require simultaneous or iterative calculation; their connections are not necessarily a one-way task sequence.
Uncertainty and marginMeasurement uncertainty, manufacturing tolerance, variable conditions, model error, and unsupported assumptions kept distinct. State how a margin is calculated and what it protects. Do not silently add a factor or treat a nominal surplus as a guaranteed allowance.
Verification and acceptance argumentThe criterion, tested configuration and conditions, method, result, source, reviewer, and unresolved exceptions. Separate analysis supporting a choice, installation acceptance, integrated performance, and usefulness in operation.

Evidence supplies the parameters and configuration; inquiry identifies gaps; planning calls the applicable calculation; logistics and execution carry its prerequisites into the work; verification checks the result; memory and learning preserve the basis and test whether it transfers. People supply technical judgment, challenge assumptions, develop alternatives, and authorize the appropriate decisions throughout.

For an air handler, a replacement cassette can fit physically yet require more fan pressure at the intended airflow. AP links the cassette revision to the airflow calculation, fan and controls, installation package, test procedure, and occupancy prerequisites. A changed cassette reopens those dependent conclusions for review. It does not erase unrelated accepted work or automatically authorize a redesign.

Each model has a stated use and credibility basis: implementation checks, comparison with independent observations, known errors, applicability limits, and responsible reviewer. The model’s answer returns as supported, contradicted, or unresolved for that use. Specialist calculations exchange units, boundary conditions, configuration versions, and uncertainty; inconsistent inputs or failure to converge leave the combined answer unresolved. When performance and resource calculations interact, AP recalculates affected work until the comparison is consistent or exposes the unresolved coupling.

An engineering change identifies both what must be recalculated and what must be rechecked in the field. The responsible people decide whether remaining work can proceed, must wait, or needs another method under existing authority. Later operating observations can reopen a performance question without pretending that an earlier test covered conditions it never encountered.

Records, plans, and possibilities

AP preserves several views of the same undertaking. A statement belongs to a view and retains its provenance; a convincing simulated result cannot promote itself into an actual event.

ViewWhat it means
Recorded history and current understandingObserved work, source reports, issued decisions, commitments, and current claims about conditions. Accepted evidence can still be corrected. Conflicting or incomplete accounts remain visible.
Approved planThe authorized baseline and issued revisions. It records intended work and responsibilities, not a guarantee of future events or blanket permission to start every task.
Current forecastThe predicted result of the current course using a dated snapshot. It can diverge from the approved plan without changing instructions.
Proposed alternativesSeparate branches with their own choices and simulated resource use. Several branches can use the same hypothetical crane slot; they create no real reservation.

A relevant record includes a stable identity, scope, source and revision, author or actor, applicable units and reference frame, and access conditions. AP distinguishes when a condition applied from when the system learned about it. Historical replays use only information available at the decision time.

A correction links to the earlier record and identifies what it supersedes. A changed physical condition creates a new dated observation. Neither erases what the team knew or had already done. AP marks dependent forecasts as needing review and identifies affected instructions; it does not automatically undo completed work.

Building the model from evidence

AP begins with an objective or a consequential finding. It builds a usable model through evidence, explicit assumptions, and targeted conversations with the people who understand the work. Importing a schedule supplies part of that model, not proof that the undertaking has been fully represented.

  1. Frame the undertaking. Establish the desired result, provisional success criteria, current commitments, and decisions still open. Identify who can clarify priorities. For existing work, preserve completed quantities, incurred costs, and binding obligations.
  2. Bring in permitted evidence. Collect the relevant records, observations, and interviews. Preserve source identities, revisions, timestamps, coverage, and rights. An extraction model may draft quantities or relationships; the original source remains available, and consequential uncertainties go to a reviewer.
  3. Resolve identities and meanings. Connect drawing elements, schedule activities, purchase lines, locations, and observations to the same real objects. Distinguish design quantity, delivered quantity, and accepted installed quantity. Copies of one report do not become independent corroboration. Ambiguous matches remain proposals.
  4. Construct the outcome requirements. Work backward from success to required results and acceptance. Work forward from current conditions to the actions and resources capable of producing them. Compare those requirements with the imported plan to find omitted work, interfaces, and unresolved obligations.
  5. Propose methods and dependencies. Use comparable projects, method libraries, supplier information, and team proposals. Record why each prerequisite exists. Ask whether a sequence is physically necessary or simply customary. If known methods fail, generate variations in design, timing, resources, or division of work and qualify them before treating them as executable.
  6. Attach behavior and uncertainty. Supply quantities, productive rates, interruption rules, resource demands, calendars, acceptance checks, and responses to exceptions. Identify which inputs are measured, transferred from comparable work, supplied by an expert, or merely assumed. Use ranges or named scenarios where probabilities are unsupported.
  7. Review the parts that can change a decision. Ask the trade lead about the method, the scheduler about dependencies, the accountant about cost and cash, and the appropriate reviewer about acceptance. One person can fill all relevant roles on a small job. Check units, missing prerequisites, conflicting reservations, and double-counted scope.
  8. Issue a dated model for a stated use. Freeze the accepted inputs, unresolved assumptions, model versions, and comparison purpose. State what the model can support now, what remains conditional, and which observation is worth obtaining next.

From source records to executable work

Suppose the imported schedule contains one line: “install air handler.” AP brings together the following records to propose what that line actually requires.

EvidenceWhat AP proposes in the modelWhat still needs establishing

A delivery forecast remains separate from observed arrival and acceptance.The receiver confirms the items delivered; the installation lead checks the route and handling equipment against the actual configuration.

The resulting package can calculate when work is possible, what it consumes, and when it can be accepted. The schedule line supplies the starting scope; the evidence and team review supply its executable structure. Missing supports, an unverified route, or an unavailable specialist appear as unresolved prerequisites rather than disappearing inside a duration estimate.

AP checks model coverage by tracing each success condition to supporting work and each critical package to its prerequisites, resources, and acceptor. Uncovered requirements and unverified dependencies remain explicit. A model is ready for a particular decision when its unresolved conditions either do not change the choice across their plausible range or are clearly presented as conditions of the recommendation. The team can choose to act under unresolved uncertainty; AP records that decision rather than inventing certainty.

How the model calculates

A work package calls the behavior models appropriate to its method. A production model turns quantities, crew composition, equipment, and conditions into productive effort. Shared calendars determine when those resources coincide. Waiting, setup, restart, and acceptance are calculated separately. Physical models can evolve conditions such as strength, moisture, or equipment wear between work events.

The project engine advances through deliveries, resource releases, completed quantities, observations, decisions, and checks. Decision rules use only the information available to the modeled participant. The team can intervene in a simulated run, alter an assumption or response, and compare the consequences. Actual authority remains separate from simulated actions.

Each behavior model declares its required inputs and units, outputs, validity conditions, parameters, uncertainty, evidence basis, and version. A result identifies the resources consumed or occupied, changed state, cost basis, acceptance still needed, and assumptions used. A specialist's unresolved answer or failure remains unresolved in the parent model.

Refinement replaces a broad allowance with detailed behavior for the same scope. It must preserve completed work, resource identities, source cost allocations, and the parent's required results. A floor expanded into tasks cannot acquire a second crane or count both the original duration allowance and its replacement. Physical prerequisites must permit a feasible sequence; iterative design and rework are represented as explicit repeated decisions or events, not unexplained circular prerequisites.

AP compares alternative methods under common external scenarios, then investigates the unknowns capable of changing the choice. Greater detail is useful when it improves that comparison. Sometimes the right next step is a measurement or a conversation rather than another calculation. Whole-project consequences include cost, cash timing, resource demand, acceptance, and occupancy—not only the date of the local task.

The project engine, optimization, and specialist calculations remain exchangeable. NVIDIA tools belong where a geometric, physical, routing, or other supported calculation improves a decision. They do not supply the entire project behavior or determine who may act.

How the capabilities work together

The eleven capabilities are responsibilities within one system, not eleven mandatory services or separate databases. Each uses the shared identities and handoff rules. Authority and human interfaces apply throughout; learning and verification can occur before, during, or after execution.

CapabilityReads and contributes
Intent and authorityTurns expressed intent into versioned objectives, criteria, and priorities. Records grants and checks who may make each consequential change.
Telemetry — Eyes and EarsUses objectives, dependencies, and open questions to establish an observation plan. Supplies observations, source health, missing coverage, and requested checks.
Field observation and evidenceTests what observations and records establish. Contributes supported findings, conflicting claims, uncertainty, and questions still requiring evidence.
Project and asset modelConnects assets, work, claims, resources, and decisions. Maintains dated views and identifies what depends on a changed input.
Reasoning and inquiryChooses consequential questions and methods. Identifies who needs the answer, when it matters, and when to investigate, act, wait, or stop.
General planning and simulationDevelops and qualifies alternatives, calculates consequences, and proposes a course with its assumptions and conditions. It does not create real commitments.
Resources and logisticsEstablishes actual availability, holds, and commitments. Allocates shared capacity through authorized actions and supplies readiness to the plan.
Work coordination and executionCarries a released instruction to its executor, follows acknowledgment and exceptions, and records performance and resource use.
Verification and acceptanceCompares results with scoped criteria. Records accepted, failed, or unresolved requirements and the specific downstream work each result affects.
Project memory and learningPreserves and retrieves project history and reasoning. Compares predictions, explanations, and outcomes, retaining why cases were investigated and lessons accepted or rejected. Proposes changes to models, methods, observation plans, or practices with their evidence and limits.
Human and agent interfacesLets people and authorized agents inspect, correct, propose, decide, and report through the same scoped model. Shows the information and actions appropriate to each role.

Integration carries permitted records and authorized updates between AP and other platforms. It preserves the meaning and ownership of each record across these responsibilities. A source system's “approved” label cannot substitute for distinguishing technical acceptance, a financial commitment, and permission to start.

Business management in the shared model

People establish whose objectives a comparison serves and how to resolve competing interests. AP links each alternative to affected parties, costs, cash needs, benefits, and obligations. Performance measures refer to the same underlying work and evidence, with explicit definitions and reporting boundaries. A portfolio view combines shared resources and consequences without duplicating commitments or counting the same benefit twice.

A changed forecast can open a project decision and a portfolio allocation decision at once. AP identifies both owners and shows the consequences; authority and existing commitments still govern action. What people see and control

The interface makes the model's distinctions visible. A person can inspect current evidence, compare it with the approved plan, and explore an alternative without silently changing live work. A proposed change shows what differs, why, who is affected, and what committing would authorize.

  • For a solo task: the next useful action, the reason for a question, and the outcome check. Recording a self-check does not imply independent verification.
  • For a foreman: current released instructions, ready work, missing prerequisites, and a quick way to report an exception or result. A changed revision shows what changed.
  • For a planner: approved plan, current forecast, alternative branches, assumptions, shared-resource conflicts, and the consequences of a proposed response.
  • For a decision-maker: the comparison, evidence, unresolved conditions, last useful decision time, exact commitment requested, and authority needed.
  • For an operator or later owner: asset history, outstanding obligations, maintenance information, and the reasons behind decisions that still affect the asset.

A correction creates a reviewed change to the relevant claim or assumption and shows its affected forecasts. Selecting an alternative creates a proposal for authorization. Issuing an instruction uses a separate command. Pending, failed, and unconfirmed saves remain visible, including during offline work.

Shared handoff rules

Implementation rules supporting this behavior. The sections above describe what people and capabilities do. The rules below specify what information must travel between components so that revisions, authority, and results keep their meaning. Storage and service boundaries can evolve while preserving these rules.

HandoffRequired content and behavior
Evidence into the modelStable source and record IDs, applicable scope and revision, observed and received times, units, method, uncertainty, and access conditions. Repeated delivery creates no second observation. Corrections retain their relationship to the original.
Model into a calculationA dated input snapshot, question, scenario, model versions, calendars, resource state, assumptions, and required outputs. A simulation uses its own branch and cannot issue real-world commands.
Calculation into a proposalResult, validity conditions, uncertainty, changed resources and costs, affected requirements, and the inputs it depended on. A later relevant correction marks the result stale until reviewed or recalculated.
Proposal into a commandExact proposal revision and scope, authenticated actor, current authority, expected record version, and a stable operation ID. Recheck shared capacity and allowances at commitment. Retries must not create duplicate reservations or actions.
Command into another platformPersist the authorized operation and delivery request together. Retain its external reference and receipt. An uncertain outcome remains unconfirmed; reconcile it before retrying a potentially binding action or releasing its allocation.
Execution into acceptancePerformed scope, actual quantities and times, evidence, relevant instruction and criterion revisions, and acceptor. Partial acceptance releases only the covered prerequisites.

At the boundary between the application and core services, the application and database own authorized record changes and consequential commands. Core analysis and simulation return evidence-linked proposals through agreed interfaces. Human entry, an external connector, and an AI recommendation must ultimately respect the same command rules.

When an upstream finding is withdrawn after work begins, preserve performed work and incurred costs. Identify affected remaining work and acceptance, notify the responsible coordinator, and follow the authorized hold, review, or resume procedure. An analysis result does not itself command a crew to stop or resume.

Unavailable models or connectors leave the manual path usable. AP shows dated evidence and unresolved conditions rather than inventing a fresh result. A stale permission cannot authorize a new consequential action.

How learning changes the model

Memory remains useful even when no lesson is accepted. The retrieval process searches permitted records and follows their links to recover an answer with dated sources, recorded reasons, and unresolved gaps. Its search index is derived and rebuildable; underlying records and current access govern the answer. A lesson review then records whether that history warrants a change and why, including rejected and deferred proposals.

Each prediction keeps the input revisions, method, assumptions, decision-time information, and expected result. Execution adds actual productive effort, waiting, rework, resource use, cost, and acceptance. Matching totals alone do not validate the components: slower assembly and faster alignment can cancel in the total.

AP first distinguishes a wrong starting state, a wrong behavior model, a changed condition, a different human response, and a misleading observation. A corrected delivery assignment updates this job. A repeated rate difference under comparable conditions may justify a model change. An interview supplies an explanation to investigate, not automatic proof of cause.

A proposed general model change has its own version, evidence, applicability, and evaluation against separate cases. Promotion into future forecasts follows the team's delegated review rules; earlier versions and predictions remain recoverable. If later results deteriorate, AP can return to the prior model while preserving the reason. A person's preference, a shared procedure, and decision authority are maintained separately.

Learning can change which signals AP watches, which questions it asks, and when it stops asking redundant questions. Changes remain within collection and participation permissions. Better performance does not grant AP additional authority. An unchosen strategy remains a simulated comparison even when the selected strategy succeeds.

Data and workflow ownership

The client controls permitted use, sharing, retention, and export. Access applies to source records and their derived findings, forecasts, and interview interpretations. Cross-project learning requires permission to use the relevant histories; it is not an automatic consequence of storing them.

Each workflow has a designated authoritative writer for its scope. AP can connect reviewed exports from Procore, Raken, and Sage while those tools retain their records. Moving a workflow requires an explicit owner, reconciled open state, a switch point, and a clear place for the next official entry. Moving the workflow does not transfer human decision authority.

Where teams cannot share underlying evidence, they can exchange an authorized readiness statement with scope, issuer, revision, current state, expiry, and a contact for clarification. Missing or expired clearance cannot become “ready,” and the statement must not expose private detail through its explanation.

A portable project record includes permitted evidence, stable identifiers and relationships, decisions, input snapshots, model references and assumptions, and outstanding obligations. A link to a document the next platform cannot retrieve is insufficient. Restricted components and any resulting replay limits remain explicit.

Testing the whole architecture

The seven examples test different demands on the same model. Each acceptance case specifies inputs, expected state changes, and observable outcomes. The checks below define the required behavior; they do not report test results.

ExampleWhat the architecture must demonstrate
Clogged sinkOne person can begin, act, self-check, and retain the result without mandatory project or team setup. An inconclusive check leaves the relevant question open.
’67 ChevyA revised preference changes the affected criterion and instructions while preserving the earlier reasoning and allowing independent work to continue.
Attached garageA proposed change reaches its relevant physical dependencies. Missing survey evidence remains visible rather than producing unsupported design certainty.
Duplex portfolioEvidence transfers only within supported conditions and access rights. A local mismatch changes the local recommendation without erasing the broader inquiry.

Cross-cutting checks include repeated imports, concurrent reservations, a superseded instruction, withdrawn permission, offline submission, partial acceptance, inaccessible evidence, and a corrected model input. The system must preserve identity, authority, accounting, and recovery through those cases.

Correct records are necessary but not sufficient. Compare the user's effort and outcome with their ordinary process. Evaluate forecasts on work not used to tune them, include misses and unanswered questions, and measure whether advice arrived while a useful choice remained. The architecture succeeds when its shared model helps the person achieve the objective and leaves dependable knowledge for the next undertaking.
