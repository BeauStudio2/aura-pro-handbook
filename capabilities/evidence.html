Field observation and evidence

Establish what someone observed, what it settles, and what the next person still needs to check.

Inputs and outputs

Inputs
A question, earlier records, and observations from people or instruments.
Expected output
A finding that names the condition, its sources, who checked it, and which decision can rely on it. An open question goes to the person who can settle it.

Approach

Start with the decision someone faces. On a five-minute repair, the owner can run the tap and see whether the sink keeps up. On the tower, the superintendent needs the foreman’s reading before releasing the adjacent panel for installation. Once that panel goes up, changing the lift sequence means bringing the façade crew and crane back. The time to measure is while the choice is still open.

Ask what each source can establish. An issued detail shows what the designer called for. A tape reading describes the distance between two physical points at a particular time. A supplier’s dimension applies to a particular equipment configuration. A finding explains how those facts bear on the decision. Someone looking at it later should be able to reconstruct the comparison.

Measuring the tower opening

Can the assembled air handler enter through one façade bay on its lifting frame? The example dimensions below illustrate how the team could settle the width question. They are assumed values for this lift.

Issued design
The current detail calls for 101 inches clear at the height where the frame passes. The project manager identifies the level, bay, section, and issued sheet revision.
Field reading
The foreman measures across the travel path at several heights and depths. An installed bracket projects into the opening. The narrowest tape reading, from the opposite jamb to the bracket face, is 99½ inches. The foreman records the endpoints, tape position, time, and photos in Raken and estimates a ¼-inch uncertainty from seating and reading the tape in that position.
Equipment
The supplier identifies the actual frame revision and confirms that the assembly is at least 100 inches wide in the orientation on the lift plan, including its projecting fittings. The bare unit’s catalog width would answer a different question.
Finding
Even at the upper end of the field estimate, the opening is 99¾ inches. The frame needs at least 100 inches before allowing any room to maneuver. Width alone rules out the planned straight pass. The foreman radios the superintendent, who holds the affected lift and sends the crew to the next approved bay.

If the plausible widths overlap, the comparison stays open. The lift lead identifies which dimension needs a better reading and who can obtain it before the panel goes up. For a wider opening, the team also checks height, obstructions through the depth of the opening, and the room the rigged load needs as it moves. Spare width at one face can settle only part of that check.

When the drawing and field disagree

  1. Check that both records describe the same condition. The project manager confirms the issued sheet, bay, elevation, and dimension endpoints. A grid-to-grid dimension could coexist with a smaller clear opening. Here the detail explicitly calls for clear width at the bracket. The lift lead repeats the measurement there and confirms the obstruction. Its cause is still open.
  2. Give the discrepancy an owner. The project manager links both readings and the marked detail to a request for information (RFI) in Procore. The design reviewer answers whether the bracket stays and what detail would accommodate the lift. In this example it stays. The lift team measures the route with two adjacent panels held out and confirms the framed unit can pass. The reviewer confirms the revised opening and temporary protection detail.
  3. Update the people using the finding. Aura Pro’s proposed role is to link the official response and both observations to the bay, flag the one-panel plan as infeasible, and send the checked alternative to the scheduler. The original drawing, observed condition, and accepted detail each retain their date and author. The scheduler can now test the October 1 opening forecast. The GC executive owns the $4,000 decision and the superintendent owns the field release. Tower spending and crew release

A corrected tape reading supersedes the earlier reading and records why. Moving the bracket later creates a new condition with its own observation time. Either change prompts a review of the findings and open instructions that depend on that clearance. After the lift, the foreman records which frame actually passed through which opening. Years later, facilities can recover that evidence and identify what a replacement lift needs measured again.

How the tools fit today

The client uses Raken, Procore, and Sage. Confirm the active connectors and the project’s issued-drawing register with the team. The following is a proposed fit for that toolset.

Raken
The foreman owns the field entry. Use the signed report and photos from the configured Procore Documents sync, or a reviewed export. Report sync follows signature. An urgent hold travels by radio while there is still time to act. The original entry and its copied PDF count as one observation. A second person taking a new reading has a separate observation. Raken report sync
Procore
The project manager tracks the RFI against the drawing. The designated RFI manager marks the design reviewer’s official response in Procore. Link the issued sheet revision and the field measurement so the reviewer can see which detail changed. Procore official responses
Sage
Accounting owns the posted cost of the change. Aura Pro can link the posting back to the clearance finding so someone can explain the cost. Geometry comes from the lift evidence. Sage posting in the tower example

What Aura Pro takes over

First, connect the evidence. Aura Pro owns the comparison, its sources, and the outstanding question. Record the project, source record and revision, observer, location, method, units, observed time, and time received. Link copies by their source identifiers. A file checksum can confirm identical bytes. Two differently rendered reports with uncertain origins go to the project manager to match before either counts as corroboration.

Then, take over field capture where the crew adopts it. The app already has permission-checked media upload and retrieval. Extend its capture form to guide the foreman through endpoints, orientation, readings, and uncertainty. Keep authorized originals in private object storage and revisioned observations and source links in PostgreSQL. Give each offline entry a stable ID and show it as pending until its upload is confirmed. A retry uses the same ID. If a model reads the tape photo, retain the proposed value alongside the image for the observer to check.

Transfer authorship by crew and record type. When a crew can capture, correct, and retrieve a measurement in Aura Pro and the project accepts its report, designate it as the original for that crew’s clearance checks. Send a referenced copy to the project record. Other crews can keep originating theirs in Raken. Aura Pro can also host discrepancy review when the design team designates it for official responses and can carry over open RFIs, revisions, and reviewer permissions. The reviewer still owns the technical answer.

Client walkthrough

Have a foreman and reviewer reconstruct a completed clearance check from the report, photo, supplier revision, issued sheet, and response. Ask each to point to the tightest spot and explain which configuration was checked. Then replay it with a copied PDF, a missing unit, and a revised lifting frame. The copy should add no independent reading. The missing unit should send a question to the observer. The frame revision should reopen the fit comparison and notify the lift lead before the old finding is used again.
