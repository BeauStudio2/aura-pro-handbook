How the capabilities work together

The business management view uses these same capabilities to connect objectives, costs and benefits, performance measures, and portfolio decisions. People shape the priorities and alternatives throughout Mission Logic; AP supports their reasoning and follows the consequences.

These groups provide a reading order. In use, the capabilities work together and revisit one another as conditions change. Authority, learning and human interfaces apply throughout the work. Verification can settle a design trial or a field question long before the project is complete.

Consider the shipment in Telemetry. Tomorrow’s installation needs its material on the correct floor. One missing scan brings several capabilities into the same decision:

  1. Establish the need and what to watch. Intent and authority records the installation objective and who may change the sequence. General planning and simulation identifies the delivery dependency. Telemetry watches its progress and identifies the missing floor scan.
  2. Resolve the useful question. Reasoning and inquiry identifies the check that could change the crew’s next action. Telemetry obtains the receiver’s confirmation that the pallet is downstairs. Field observation and evidence establishes which pallet and location the report supports. The project and asset model connects that finding to the shipment, floor and installation task.
  3. Compare and authorize the response. Resources and logistics identifies an available hoist slot; the lift lead confirms the load and handling time fit. Planning compares that lift with starting the crew on other ready work. The superintendent approves the revised sequence under the applicable authority.
  4. Carry out and check the work. Work coordination and execution carries the instruction to the lift lead and follows its acknowledgment and completion. Telemetry brings back the arrival record. Verification and acceptance records the installation lead’s check that the required quantity is present and usable before the crew starts.
  5. Improve the next decision. Project memory and learning compares forecast and actual timing and examines whether the missing information warrants a different observation plan. Across these exchanges, human and agent interfaces give each participant the evidence, question and permitted action relevant to their role.

The connections work in both directions. A new finding updates the project model and can reopen an inquiry or invalidate a plan. A simulation identifies an assumption to observe. An acceptance check can trigger corrective work. Learning can add a useful watch or remove a redundant question. Integration with other platforms carries the source records and approved updates across those connections.

The first client application

On a small repair, one person can describe the problem, do the work, and check the result. On a development, the field crew, scheduler, and project executive each need a different part of the same decision. The chapters use that setting to explain what each part of Aura Pro contributes.

The construction client already uses Raken for field reports, Procore for project decisions, and Sage for accounting. Aura Pro connects a finding in the field to the plan, the person authorized to act, and the instruction the crew receives. A workflow can move into Aura Pro when the client designates it as the place to do and record that work.
