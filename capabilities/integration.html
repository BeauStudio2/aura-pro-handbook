Construction tools and integration

Construction is the first client application. This guide describes how Aura Pro connects the tools the team uses today and takes on specific workflows as the client adopts them.

What Aura Pro owns

The client uses Procore, Raken, and Sage. Start with the records and screens people already trust. Aura Pro owns the intent, stable project and asset identities, cross-system relationships, evidence behind each recommendation, and the handoff to the next decision maker. Assign one authoritative writer per record type and project. Confirm the client’s Procore Estimating access, Raken production records, Sage edition, and enabled connections before relying on them.

A field observation describes a condition. A technical review records a judgment about a design. An authorization permits a scoped action. A commitment reserves money or resources. A posting records an accounting event. A forecast estimates a consequence. Preserve those meanings across every adapter, even if two source systems label their records “approved.”

The three client tools

Tool Initial use and integration route What Aura Pro does
Procore The estimator measures the issued drawings in Procore Estimating and checks revisions. The project team uses Procore for formal design answers and approved changes. Procore drawing takeoffs and revisions Aura Pro links each reviewed quantity and decision to its drawing, revision, and scope. If the estimating module is unavailable, the estimator supplies a checked takeoff from the issued sheets.

Estimating new work from past projects

A contractor is estimating concrete work for a new office building using records from a completed office building with comparable slabs. This fictional worked example shows how AP connects past production to a new estimate. Both projects, their records, quantities, rates and prices are invented for the example; they do not describe a client project or current market pricing.

From a drawing to a priced item

  1. The estimator measures the new building’s issued drawings. In Procore Estimating, measure each slab area and apply its specified thickness to get concrete volume. Check thickened edges and openings against the details. Take rebar sizes, spacing, and laps from the schedules and notes. The estimator reviews those quantities, compares each addendum with the prior sheets, and records the affected lines. Aura Pro links each accepted quantity to its sheet and revision. Procore drawing and revision workflow
  2. Aura Pro reconstructs the completed project’s rate. Match Raken crew hours and accepted installed quantities for the same activity, area, and dates. Check Procore for revisions and scope changes. Reconcile costs to the corresponding Sage job and cost code. For example, 160 labor-hours spent placing 200 installed cubic yards gives 0.8 labor-hours per cubic yard. If the timecard also includes formwork or rebar, the estimator must resolve that allocation before using the placement rate.
  3. The estimator prices the new building’s concrete work. Suppose the reviewed drawing quantity is 250 cubic yards of comparable slab. At the completed project’s rate, placement would take 200 labor-hours. Price those hours at the new project’s wage and burden. Price material separately using the purchase quantity, including any documented order allowance. Show formwork and rebar under their own units and activities. The estimator reviews differences in access, reinforcement congestion, and crew mix, then accepts or adjusts the rate and records the reason.

A fictional pricing run

Assume placement labor costs $70 per labor-hour including burden. The 200-hour estimate gives $14,000 in placement labor. For this example only, the estimator allows 4% above the 250-cubic-yard design quantity when ordering concrete: 260 cubic yards at an assumed delivered price of $180 per cubic yard gives $46,800 in concrete. The combined subtotal is $60,800. This covers placement labor and delivered concrete only; formwork, reinforcing steel, pump hire, finishing, testing and applicable taxes require separate estimate lines. The assumed allowance and prices are inputs to review, not general estimating rules.

Now simulate an addendum increasing the comparable slab quantity from 250 to 300 cubic yards. At the same provisional placement rate, AP recalculates 240 labor-hours, an increase of 40. The purchasing quantity and affected costs also change. AP sends the revised comparison to the estimator, who checks whether the added scope still supports the same rate and allowance before accepting it. The planner separately checks crew and delivery capacity; 40 more labor-hours do not by themselves establish a completion-date delay.

How to test whether the quantities are usable

  1. Choose a package from the completed project with an issued drawing set, one known addendum, and an estimator-reviewed takeoff. Agree accuracy tolerances by element and unit, which omissions would affect the bid, and how much review time is acceptable. Have the estimator complete the takeoff before seeing the reference quantities.
  2. Run the original issue, then the addendum. Compare baseline quantities and revision changes line by line. Record missing, extra, and duplicated elements, signed and absolute errors, and estimator minutes spent correcting or mapping the export. Check unchanged items too. A missing wall and an overstated slab can cancel in the total.
  3. Trace each retained quantity to its sheet, location, unit, activity, and cost code. Record the share that can be matched to recorded work on the completed project and the share that remains unresolved. Repeat on an untouched zone. Accept the source for a scope only if it meets the agreed tolerances and review budget there. Compare the resulting estimate for the new project with an independent estimate at the same scope and price date. This last comparison tests the rate transfer as well as the quantities.

What the estimate needs from Aura Pro

Give each quantity a basis and a source. A slab can have a design volume, a purchased volume, and an installed volume. Store each with its element, location, unit, activity, source revision, measurement method, and review status. This lets purchasing apply an allowance and lets estimating calculate installation productivity without counting that allowance as installed work.

Carry revisions into dependent decisions. Preserve the original export, estimator corrections, and any assumptions connecting a quantity to a productivity rate. When an addendum changes a slab, show the changed quantity, labor-hours, and cost beside the last reviewed estimate. Flag affected crew and delivery plans for review. A repeated import should produce one change. A new forecast goes to the estimator before it can change a bid or a commitment.

Keep the measuring tool replaceable. Let a workbook, model, or manual measurement enter through the same import and review process. Record who measured, who reviewed, and which revision they accepted. The historical package replay then becomes a comparison any replacement must pass. An API can automate delivery later if it preserves the same source detail.

Track the takeoff as assigned work. Record the sheets and exclusions sent, the expected return date, the received version, and who owes the next review. A partial return can support pricing the covered elements while missing elements remain open.

A cross-system handoff

  1. A foreman records a field observation in Raken. A linked Procore document can provide the same report. Aura Pro assigns one observation ID and retains both source references.
  2. Aura Pro associates it with a model or drawing revision, equipment ID, work package, and open RFI. A disputed match goes to review before it affects a plan.
  3. The planner compares available sequences. A spatial engine can flag a modeled obstruction, while a schedule engine estimates a missed commissioning slot. Both return their input versions and assumptions.
  4. Aura Pro assembles the options, cost exposure, and current delegation. The authorized person commits the selected revision in the designated record system. Reconcile that decision back to Aura Pro.
  5. Execution receives the instruction. Finance posts through its agreed Sage path. Verification records what actually happened. Later updates reference the original decision and affected work.

The integration contract

AP assigns IDs to the organization, project, asset, work package, and decision. It maps each external tenant, record type, and native ID to its AP counterpart, preserving source author, revision, currency, units, visibility, observed time, and received time. Raw evidence remains immutable under the applicable retention policy; corrections appear as linked revisions.

AP reads via supported APIs, native sync, or reviewed files and re-fetches notifications from their authoritative source. For outgoing work, it saves the proposed operation and its stable key in the same transaction as the approved change. Delivery is asynchronous and retains the acknowledgment or external reference. When delivery is uncertain, AP queries before retrying. It reconciles totals, revisions, and failed deliveries. Each connector is restricted to the tenant, project, and operations it needs. One external login or admin credential never establishes a person's right to approve work.

From integration to native workflows

Connect. Agree the authoritative writer for each object. Pick a real decision and trace its identifiers across the field report, design revision, approval, instruction, and cost posting. Count re-entry, missing references, and time awaiting a decision.

Coordinate. Aura Pro assembles the context, routes the question, and follows it through acknowledgment while the client retains its formal records. Compare decisions and downstream totals with the existing process. Surface any variance for a human to resolve.

Take ownership by workflow. After a client-designated owner confirms equivalent semantics, designate Aura Pro as writer for that workflow, migrate active state and history, and switch consumers to Aura Pro receipts. Maintain export and reconciliation. If cutover fails, stop outgoing writes, reconcile changes since cutover, and return the single-writer role explicitly. Adopt native AP workflows where they reduce effort or improve decisions.

The portable unit is the decision and its evidence. A provider can be replaced when the replacement preserves identity, revisions, authority, resource balances, and outcomes in replay. Measure approval lead time, duplicate entries, unresolved sync discrepancies, and the user's effort on comparable work.
