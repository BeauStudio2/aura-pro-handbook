Intent and authority

When should AP take a more active role, and who decides?

Define the desired result, the decisions people retain, and the work they delegate.

In Mission Logic: The team defines the objective, assigns decision rights, and names who may commit resources or release work. AP checks those permissions throughout the process.

Case: the October tower

This chapter examines one responsibility within the introductory tower decision. Its dates and decisions remain the same.

Approach

An owner can say “open the lower floors on October 1 and keep them adaptable for the next tenant.” That sets a date and a design priority. It leaves real choices open. Can the superintendent resequence work? Who accepts a design deviation? Who can spend more to preserve October 1? Capture these distinctions while the people making the agreement are present.

Intent records the outcome, priorities when they conflict, evidence of success, unresolved questions, source, owner, scope, and revision. Authority is a dated grant from a named principal to a person or role. It identifies the permitted action, area or work package, per-action and aggregate spending limits, period, prerequisites, and decisions reserved to someone else. The principal confirms the grant. The project administrator maps the named delegate to the account they use to act.

Start proportionately. One person doing a repair can set the goal, do the work, and accept a functional check. In a tower, the owner can reserve a change in occupancy strategy, the designer can review a routing change, and the superintendent can control crew sequencing. Record the actual delegation for this project. A job title alone supplies too little evidence to infer it.

From quiet observation to recommendations

People shape the objective and the alternatives before a permission is needed. AP helps them explore consequences and explain competing priorities. The business management view connects these choices to beneficiaries, payers, and the parties whose agreement is required.

Learning can make AP more useful. A named person with the relevant authority decides whether its role changes. Permission to observe, ask a question, recommend a course, issue an instruction and commit money each has its own scope. In a solo job, that person can be the person doing the work.

Consider the concrete deliveries in Project memory and learning. In this example, the general contractor (GC) project executive authorizes the superintendent to set AP's observation and recommendation role for those deliveries. The superintendent initially permits AP to read selected tickets, dispatch records and pump logs, save forecasts quietly, and bring questions to an agreed review with the dispatcher. AP does not send unsolicited dispatch recommendations during the pour. Existing operational reporting and agreed immediate-response channels continue.

After reviewing its performance, the superintendent can permit AP to raise release-or-hold recommendations directly with the dispatcher for the named pours and shifts. The permission identifies the sources, situations worth raising, recipient and backup, channel, effective period and person who may amend it. The dispatcher acknowledges the arrangement. AP shows the evidence, alternatives and last useful decision time. The dispatcher still chooses whether to release the truck. Changing the schedule, instructing a crew or spending money requires the applicable separate authority.

How the team decides AP is ready

Before reviewing results, the superintendent and dispatcher agree what would make recommendations useful for this work. For example: truck assignments must be checked, the recommendation must reach the dispatcher before the release decision closes, and unresolved inputs must be visible. They also agree how much unnecessary interruption is acceptable and which misses would prevent expanded use. These criteria belong to the proposed responsibility.

AP assembles its saved forecasts and proposed messages alongside actual deliveries and the dispatcher's decisions. The dispatcher checks operational relevance; the superintendent reviews readiness against the agreed criteria. They examine ordinary deliveries, disruptions, false alarms and missed problems, including work not used to adjust the model. They compare with the team's existing practice. A simulated alternative remains labeled as simulated; one successful delivery does not establish its superiority.

The superintendent records the decision: retain quiet observation, permit recommendations within a narrower scope, or allow the proposed role. The record names the evidence reviewed, unresolved weaknesses, scope, start time and next review date. The superintendent confirms the scoped permission. In this example, the dispatcher's acknowledgment is required before activation. AP shows the permission as pending until both are recorded and the start time has arrived; it then shows the role as active if the grant is still valid. A favorable AP assessment alone changes no permission. Later results return to Learning's evaluation of usefulness.

Reducing or withdrawing authority

The superintendent, or another person authorized to amend the grant, can narrow the permitted pours, recipients or sources, return AP to review-only participation, or withdraw the role. This can happen at any time, including when the team changes its working arrangements. The dispatcher can request a reduction; direct control to pause recommendations can also be delegated explicitly.

Suppose a changed ticket process causes repeated assignment errors. The superintendent returns AP to quiet observation for affected deliveries and records the reason and effective time. Withdrawal takes effect at its recorded effective time without waiting for the recipient's acknowledgment. AP stops new proactive recommendations in that scope, cancels queued sends and recurring prompts that depend on the withdrawn permission, and shows the changed role to the dispatcher. It may continue collecting records and evaluating forecasts only within the permissions still in force.

A recommendation already delivered remains in the history. Its record shows that AP's permission to make further recommendations has ended; that does not by itself withdraw the earlier advice. If the superintendent or another authorized reviewer withdraws or supersedes that advice, the record identifies the affected recommendation, reason and any replacement, and the dispatcher receives the update. If the dispatcher has already released a truck, withdrawing AP's role does not cancel that action. The dispatcher checks the current situation and issues any revised direction. The same distinction applies to an issued crew instruction or an executed commitment. Restoring AP's active role requires a new authorized decision based on reviewed evidence; improved scores do not restore it automatically.

Who decides on the tower

The façade crew is about to install the panel beside the opening reserved for an air handler. Can the superintendent leave both panels out and commit $4,000 for temporary weather protection? In this worked example the GC bears the cost. The roles, grants, and Sage configuration are illustrative.

  1. The owner’s representative sets the objective. Brief revision 3 calls for lower-floor occupancy on October 1 and access through this bay for future refits. The superintendent identifies the last lift before the crew closes the bay. Aura Pro attaches that decision deadline to the brief.
  2. The foreman calls in the clearance conflict. The foreman records the clear opening and a photo in Raken. The supplier confirms the width of the unit on its lifting frame. One panel leaves too little clearance. The foreman radios the superintendent, who holds the affected lift and directs the crew to the next approved bay. Aura Pro links the measurement, supplier confirmation, drawing revision, and Procore design question. Raken’s daily report can reach Procore Documents after signature. That copy points back to the same measurement. Raken report sync
  3. The project manager prepares the change in Procore. The lift team checks the two-panel opening against the frame. The design reviewer confirms the opening and protection detail. The façade subcontractor quotes a fixed $4,000. The scheduler finds that assembling the unit indoors misses the booked tests and moves the opening forecast to October 22. Protecting the wider opening keeps the September 30 test finish and October 1 occupancy forecast. Aura Pro presents both paths against the brief and links the reviewed detail and quote to the change event.
  4. The GC project executive commits the money. The superintendent may authorize $5,000 per temporary-work change and $15,000 in total. Earlier commitments use $12,000, leaving $3,000. Aura Pro routes the full $4,000 to the project executive, who has a separate grant to sign for the GC. The executive approves the expense and signs the subcontract change for the GC. The façade subcontractor’s signatory countersigns. The project manager records the executed commitment change order in Procore. Aura Pro matches its scope, amount, and revision to the proposal. An owner-funded change would also follow the applicable prime-contract approval route. Procore change-order routes
  5. The superintendent releases the crew. With the reviewed detail and executed change in hand, the superintendent issues the revised field instruction through Procore and radios the foreman. It identifies the two panels to hold, where to install protection, and when to close the opening after the lift. The foreman reads back the panel IDs and revision. Aura Pro tracks that acknowledgment. The foreman records protection, lift, and close-in as they occur in Raken. The scheduler uses actual progress to update the commissioning and occupancy forecast.
  6. The project accountant posts the commitment in Sage. In the Sage 300 CRE example, the subcontract is already synced. The accountant checks the job and cost code, accepts the approved change for export under the configured Procore workflow, and verifies that posting the Sage batch increased committed cost by $4,000. Aura Pro links that posting to the executed change. If export fails, the accountant corrects and reconciles it. Invoice approval and payment have their own records. Procore’s Sage 300 CRE connector

When the owner changes the goal. If the owner withdraws phased occupancy, version the brief and identify commitments justified by the earlier date. Review pending dependent proposals. If the instruction has already reached the crew, the superintendent checks what is in place and issues any revised direction to the foreman for acknowledgment. Preserve the original rationale and any cost already committed.

What Aura Pro takes over

Moving an official workflow into AP is separate from granting it authority to decide or act. The team can issue and acknowledge instructions in AP while the superintendent retains every crew-release decision. Any expansion of AP's autonomy requires its own delegation.

Coordination. When the client starts with connected tools, Aura Pro owns the brief, links to source records, and routing. It can flag the superintendent’s apparent $3,000 balance and chase a missing signature or crew acknowledgment. Procore is where the team verifies and executes the subcontract change. A commitment entered there between imports can change the balance.

Field instructions. Once the superintendent and foreman can issue, acknowledge, revise, and retrieve an instruction in Aura Pro, the GC can designate it as the official place to issue sequence changes. Transfer open instructions and their latest acknowledgments. From that point the superintendent issues the instruction there, the foreman acknowledges it there, and Procore receives a linked copy. The superintendent still decides when to release the crew.

Delegated spending. This is a separate transfer. The GC identifies every route that can bind it against the grant, reconciles open commitments and reservations, and designates one authorization check for that balance. A Procore or manual change using that grant must first obtain a reservation for the exact amount and revision, or the GC must close that route. While an independent route can draw on the same allowance, Aura Pro can report discrepancies and escalate requests. It can enforce the aggregate only once every binding route uses the check. Contract signatures can still live in Procore and posting in Sage. Workflow transfer and reversal

How permissions and spending limits are enforced

For observation and recommendations, AP checks the current permission before collecting from a source, opening a request or sending a message. A queued message retains its scope and permission reference, and the sender rechecks that permission at dispatch. A withdrawn, expired or narrowed grant blocks actions it no longer covers, including retries. If current authority cannot be established, the affected action waits. This enforces participation within AP; instructions and commitments already issued through other tools still require follow-up by their responsible people.

The TypeScript/TanStack app uses Supabase Auth and PostgreSQL. Its decision command checks organization approval rights and locks the individual request. Two requests against the same allowance have different request records, so the command also locks their shared allowance when reserving funds. The checks below keep each decision within the current grant and available balance.

Acceptance and client walkthrough

Walk through a change in AP's role. Start with quiet observation and verify that a saved recommendation cannot send itself. Authorize recommendations for one pour and dispatcher, then check that an unrelated pour remains outside the grant. Queue a recommendation, withdraw its permission before dispatch, and verify that neither the send nor a later retry proceeds. Repeat with a narrower scope and an expired grant. Confirm that the dispatcher sees the change, that an already-issued truck release remains visible for human follow-up, and that improved model results cannot reactivate the permission. These acceptance checks verify the workflow’s permission boundaries.

Test the same two requests on both sides of the transfer. Give the superintendent $3,000 remaining and submit two $2,000 changes at once. Try allowing a direct Procore approval to bypass Aura Pro’s spending check during transfer. Both could pass and exceed the grant by $1,000. With both routes using the shared reservation, the first reserves $2,000 and the second goes to the executive. A changed quote requires approval of the new revision. Revoking the grant must block a decision from an already-open screen. Losing the receipt after signature must lead back to the existing commitment.

Verify the client’s actual workflow. Choose one completed change. Ask the foreman for the field finding, the GC signatory for the grant and executed change, the superintendent for the instruction the crew acknowledged, and the accountant for the posted entry. Trace the revision and amount across those records. Confirm the Sage edition, export route, and any other way the same spending authority can be exercised.
