Verification and acceptance

Determine what a specific check establishes and who may accept the result.

On this pageInputs and outputsApproachHow it worksTechnology choicesIntegration and native ownershipWhere this capability is usedConnected capabilities

Inputs and outputs

Inputs
Agreed criteria, reported work, observed checks, unresolved exceptions, and reviewer authority.
Expected output
A scoped acceptance, a failed check requiring action, or an explicit unknown.

Across the architecture

Construction tools, data ownership, and migration ↗

Approach

Tie acceptance to a specific requirement, location, revision, evidence method, and authorized reviewer. A passing dimensional check, a design review, and permission to commission have different scopes. A failed check calls for corrective work; a missing check calls for someone to perform it.

How it works

Receive the measured clearance and supplier dimensions. The reviewer records what they establish. After installation, collect the required installation or functional evidence; commissioning and occupancy each follow their applicable acceptance path. A later correction can reopen a dependent decision while preserving what was accepted and known on the earlier date.

Technology choices

Use versioned criteria and check results in PostgreSQL, access-controlled object storage for evidence, and deterministic comparisons for units and tolerances. Link client inspection records from the designated Procore project workflow. Alternatives include a native mobile checklist and reviewed inspector-signed document import. Use recognition models for draft extraction with source references. Supabase row-level security

Integration and native ownership

Aura Pro can first assemble review packets and later own acceptance for explicitly assigned checks. Keep external professional or agency decisions with the appropriate issuer and retain their references. Test wrong-floor evidence, an unperformed check, and a superseded acceptance. None should silently release adjoining work.
