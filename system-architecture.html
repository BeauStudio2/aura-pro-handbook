System architecture

How can a field observation change a whole plan without losing the evidence, the people’s intent, or control of the work?

AP connects its capabilities through one shared model of the undertaking. The model keeps the objective, physical conditions, work, resources, questions and decisions connected. People use different views of that same account.

One undertaking, several useful views

The owner needs to know whether the building can open. The scheduler needs a workable sequence. The foreman needs a current instruction and a way to report what is different on site. Their views refer to the same equipment, work and evidence, so a correction can reach everyone whose decision it affects.

A Mission names an outcome and its accountable person. Tasks describe executable work. A work package groups scope for planning and calculation; it can contain smaller packages. Physical dependencies, shared resources and acceptance conditions connect these groups. A crane serving two trades has one capacity; showing it in two views does not create another crane.

The same structure can be small. A sink repair needs an objective, an observation, an action and an outcome check. It does not require a company, team or elaborate project hierarchy. Complexity follows the work.

Build understanding from evidence

A supplier update says an item will arrive later. AP first establishes which item, order and revision the message concerns. A forecast delivery date remains different from an observed arrival. A confident sentence or two copies of one report do not supply independent proof.

AP combines records, observations and human explanations into supported findings and explicit assumptions. A person can correct a match or explain a condition the records miss. Consequential ambiguity becomes a focused question. The resulting model retains the sources, when the condition applied, when AP learned of it, and what remains unresolved.

For example, six trucks are at a site, but two serve a different concrete pour. Receiving records and the dispatcher connect four loads to the relevant work. AP corrects the supply calculation, then advances it as time passes. A later contradiction reopens only the affected questions.

Develop and compare possible paths

People and AP work backward from success and forward from current conditions. They draw on reviewed methods, comparable work and team experience to propose alternatives. AP can combine methods or vary the sequence, resources and design; the team checks whether the resulting option can work.

Calculations use a dated model in a separate scenario. They account for quantities, effort, resource availability, waiting, physical behavior and acceptance. Alternatives can reserve the same hypothetical crane slot in separate scenarios; they do not make real bookings.

A changed input reaches the calculations that depend on it. Local progress improves the whole forecast only when it changes a controlling dependency. Detail earns its place when it can change a choice: simple arithmetic may settle clearance; a turning path may need geometry; many competing deliveries may need a dynamic simulation.

Turn a choice into coordinated work

The shared model distinguishes recorded conditions, the approved plan, the current forecast and proposed alternatives. Exploring a better sequence does not silently change a crew’s instruction.

People select a course within their authority. A spending commitment, a resource reservation and permission to begin work are separate decisions. Before release, the responsible person checks actual readiness. AP carries the issued instruction to its recipient and follows acknowledgment, exceptions, completion and acceptance.

When an input changes after work begins, AP preserves what was performed and spent. It identifies the affected remaining work and tells the people who can respond. A delayed or failed connection remains visible; retrying an operation must not create a second commitment. Manual work remains possible when an analysis service is unavailable.

Keep useful knowledge with the undertaking

Every result returns to the same account: what was expected, what happened, what the check established, and why the team chose its response. Future readers can recover the reasons behind the work, including rejected alternatives and unresolved questions.

Learning changes the appropriate thing. A corrected assignment updates this project. Repeated evidence may change a numerical forecast. A reviewed lesson can change a procedure or observation plan. A team member can revise AP’s understanding of how they prefer to work. None of these automatically expands AP’s authority.

The client controls access and permitted reuse. Records can remain in existing tools while AP connects them; a function moves only with a clear owner and handoff. Portable history includes its permitted evidence and relationships, not merely links that cease to work after a platform change.

What the architecture makes possible

The shared model lets a small observation improve a large decision without replacing the people who understand or own the work. It also exposes the limits: a missing source, an unsupported assumption or an unconfirmed commitment remains visible.

That structure makes AP’s reasoning inspectable. Whether it improves the result depends on useful evidence, credible models, timely decisions and the effort required to maintain them. The next chapter asks who benefits and how that value is assessed.
